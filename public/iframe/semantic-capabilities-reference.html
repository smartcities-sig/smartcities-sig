Semantic Capabilities Reference

All 14 semantic capabilities from semantic-capability-assessment.md, grouped by their 4 categories, with practical classification guidance from stage-1-to-stage-2-classification.md. Purpose and Examples are sourced from the framework document; How to Use It reflects the Step 2 discriminators and tie-breakers in the classification procedure.

Domain Semantics 3 capabilities

Describes what the municipality ultimately cares about by defining the service being delivered, the infrastructure providing it, and the resources consumed to achieve the desired outcome.

CapabilityPurposeHow to use itExamples
Service Outcome The real-world outcome the municipality intends to achieve, independent of how the underlying infrastructure behaves. Use when the sub-observation describes an effect experienced by people, infrastructure, or the environment — the "so what," not the infrastructure's own behavior or what it cost to produce. Street illuminance at road surface level; soil moisture at root level; water pressure delivered to consumers.
Infrastructure Output The physical behavior or output produced by infrastructure assets while delivering a service, distinguished from whether the service objective was actually achieved. Use when the sub-observation is about what the infrastructure itself is doing or producing — not whether that produced the intended real-world effect. Lumens emitted by a luminaire; pump flow rate; valve opening percentage; motor speed.
Resource Consumption The resources consumed by the infrastructure to deliver the service. Use when the sub-observation is about cost or resources spent (energy, water, fuel, battery) rather than the outcome achieved or the infrastructure's output behavior. Energy; water; fuel; battery usage; active/reactive power; voltage; frequency.

Observation Semantics 4 capabilities

Describes how operational information is observed by defining where observations originate, what they represent, how they are obtained, and their temporal characteristics.

CapabilityPurposeHow to use itExamples
Observation Point Where observations originate within the IoT system and the municipal environment, and how this affects their use and interpretation. Use when the sub-observation is about where the observation's referent physically or logically sits — a position, not an actor. When the same device name could also answer "who reported it," default here as primary with Provenance as a companion, unless the sub-observation specifically contrasts sources. Luminaire; cabinet; line head; street surface; pump; valve; pipeline; root zone; weather station.
Observation Scope Which part of the system or municipality each observation covers. Use when the sub-observation is about what portion or coverage a value represents — including a value combining multiple assets or locations into one number (space/asset aggregation, not time aggregation, which belongs to Temporal Semantics). Single asset; group of assets; cabinet; street; irrigation zone; district; entire municipality.
Observation Method How each observation (measured, estimated, predicted, simulated, aggregated across assets, proxy) is obtained. Use when the sub-observation is about the technique that produced the value — sensing vs. a computed or derived process. Never primary for an aggregation-over-time question (that's Temporal Semantics); applies as a companion when the computation itself is worth recording. Measured; estimated; inferred; predicted; aggregated (across assets/locations); simulated; model-derived; proxy measurement.
Temporal Semantics The temporal characteristics of observations and their use in KPIs and simulation. Use when the sub-observation is about what time span a value represents — including a value combining multiple readings over time into one number (e.g. a rolling average), even though it was also computed rather than directly sensed. Instantaneous; observation interval; sampling period; aggregation window; historical observation; forecast; prediction horizon.

Interpretation Semantics 4 capabilities

Describes how observations should be understood and trusted by providing the context, provenance, and quality required for meaningful operational interpretation.

CapabilityPurposeHow to use itExamples
Provenance The source of observations and operational information. Use when the sub-observation is about which system or actor reported or supplied the information — a source identity, not a location. Primary (not Observation Point) when specifically contrasting sources — device-reported vs. manually entered vs. externally supplied. Device sensor; control cabinet; weather service; GIS platform; installation records; asset management system; human operator; Digital Twin; predictive model.
Measurement Quality Accuracy, precision, reliability, completeness, and uncertainty of an observation. Use when the sub-observation is about how reliable or accurate a value is — trust conditioned on reliability/accuracy, as distinct from Observation Method's trust conditioned on production technique. Accuracy; precision; confidence; completeness; consistency; availability; resolution; uncertainty.
Operational Context Operating conditions (weather, traffic, occupancy, seasonality) that affect service and observations. Use when the sub-observation is about a dynamic, situational condition that modulates behavior in the moment. Boundary with Physical Context is dynamic vs. static — weather changes moment to moment. Weather; rainfall; humidity; temperature; wind; traffic; occupancy; seasonal conditions.
Physical Context The physical environment (buildings, trees, terrain, orientation, shadows) and its impact on service outcomes. Use when the sub-observation is about a static, structural environmental feature of the place. Boundary with Operational Context is dynamic vs. static — the tree casting a shadow is a fixed property of the location. Buildings; trees; vegetation; terrain; slopes; orientation; inclination; shadows; surface materials.

Operational Semantics 3 capabilities

Describes how municipal infrastructure is operated and managed throughout its lifecycle, including operational responsibility, communications, and resilience.

CapabilityPurposeHow to use itExamples
Asset Management Context Ownership, responsible management, contracts, warranties, maintenance history, useful life, lifecycle. Use when the sub-observation is about ownership, lifecycle, or maintenance administration — not about a specific measurement at all. Ownership; responsible organization; operational zone; maintenance responsibility; contracts; warranty; lifecycle stage; installation date; maintenance history; remaining useful life.
Network Operability Network availability, connectivity, latency, packet loss, signal quality, and communication reliability. Use when the sub-observation is about the current communication or connectivity state — is the asset reachable right now. Network availability; connectivity status; latency; packet loss; signal quality; communication reliability; communication technology.
Fallback Behaviour Autonomous behavior when communication or supervisory control is lost. Use when the sub-observation is about what the infrastructure does autonomously when supervisory control is lost. Local schedules; autonomous control; safe operating mode; emergency operating mode; manual override; local decision making.
