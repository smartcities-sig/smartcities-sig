## Municipalities Invited to Shape Practical Interoperability for Smart Cities

**Summary:** Municipalities face the challenge of selecting long-term technology, usually relying on standards and data models. However, these frameworks often fail to capture the real-world context needed to manage city services. To bridge this gap, the Smart Cities Special Interest Group (SIG) starts with the practical questions municipalities must answer, organizes them into semantic domains, and evaluates how well existing technical ecosystems support those operational needs.

### The Municipal Interoperability Challenge

Municipalities increasingly depend on connected infrastructure, digital platforms, and information systems supplied by different vendors. These systems may implement established standards and data models, yet still describe the same municipal service in different ways.

The challenge is therefore not a lack of standards.

A municipality may receive data from streetlights, water meters, environmental sensors, maintenance systems, and Digital Twin platforms. However, the availability of data does not necessarily mean that municipal systems can answer the operational questions required to manage those services.

For example:

- Is a lighting asset operating correctly?
- Is the required level of illumination being achieved?
- Is abnormal water consumption caused by demand, leakage, or a meter fault?
- What information is needed to prioritize maintenance?
- Can the same operational condition be interpreted consistently across systems from different vendors?

Answering these questions requires more than individual measurements. Municipalities also need the meaning and context necessary to interpret the information correctly.

### Starting with Operational Questions

The Smart Cities SIG begins with the operational questions municipalities need their systems and data to answer.

An operational question expresses what a municipality needs to know to operate, evaluate, maintain, or make decisions about a public service. It does not prescribe a particular technology, standard, platform, or vendor implementation.

This distinction is important. Municipalities should not need to translate their responsibilities into the terminology of each technical ecosystem before they can explain what they need. Instead, standards organizations, open source initiatives, and technology experts should be able to evaluate municipal questions through their respective models and specifications.

Related operational questions are grouped into **semantic domains**. These domains organize the analysis around common areas of meaning, such as:

- Asset identification and classification
- Operational state
- Performance and service outcomes
- Energy or resource consumption
- Faults, alarms, and abnormal conditions
- Maintenance and lifecycle management
- Location and environmental context

For each question, participants identify the information, meaning, relationships, and context required to provide a useful answer. Representatives from participating standards organizations and open initiatives can then evaluate how the question and its semantic requirements translate into their respective standards, models, and technical domains.

### A Municipality-Led Perspective

The Smart Cities SIG brings together municipalities, academia, standards organizations, open source initiatives, and industry. Each participant contributes a different form of expertise.

Municipalities define the operational questions and explain how information is used in practice. Academic participants can support the analysis and development of repeatable methods. Standards organizations and open source initiatives assess how the required concepts are represented within their own frameworks. Technology providers contribute implementation experience and identify practical integration considerations.

Municipal participation is therefore essential. Without direct municipal input, the analysis could reflect what technologies are able to provide rather than what cities need to operate their services.

The objective is not to rank standards or select a single technical solution. Each technical ecosystem remains responsible for its own specifications, models, implementations, and roadmap. The SIG provides a common municipal reference from which those ecosystems can evaluate their ability to answer the same operational questions.

### How the Analysis Works

The methodology follows a structured sequence:

| Stage | Central question | Result |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Identify operational questions | What does the municipality need to know or decide? | A set of questions grounded in municipal operations |
| Group questions into semantic domains | Which questions address related areas of meaning? | An organized framework for analysis |
| Determine required information and context | What must be represented to answer each question correctly? | Defined semantic needs independent of a specific technology |
| Assess existing models and standards | How does each ecosystem represent the required concepts? | Areas of alignment, overlap, ambiguity, or potential gaps |
| Validate with municipalities | Does the analysis reflect how the service is managed in practice? | Municipal confirmation and refinement |

This approach keeps the analysis anchored in operational needs while allowing different standards and data-model communities to participate on equal terms.

### Initial Areas of Work

The initial work focuses on public street lighting and smart water management.

These domains provide practical examples of services that depend on physical infrastructure, connected devices, operational platforms, maintenance processes, and information exchanged across organizational and technical boundaries.

They also demonstrate why data alone is not sufficient. In public lighting, electrical consumption, lamp output, asset condition, and roadway illumination are related, but they represent different operational concepts. In water management, consumption, flow, leakage, meter condition, and service events also require context before they can support operational decisions.

The purpose of examining these domains is to validate a methodology that can later be applied to other municipal services, such as:

- Irrigation
- Waste management
- Parking
- Mobility
- Environmental monitoring
- Energy management

Expansion into additional domains will depend on municipal interest and participation.

### What Municipalities Can Contribute

Municipalities do not need to arrive with a complete technical specification or formal data model. They can contribute by explaining the questions they need answered and the operational context in which those answers are used.

Possible contributions include:

- Proposing operational questions from a municipal service
- Explaining how information supports operational decisions
- Identifying terminology used by municipal teams
- Reviewing how questions are grouped into semantic domains
- Validating the information and context needed to answer them
- Reviewing the analysis of a relevant service area
- Nominating an operational or technical expert for selected working sessions

Participation can be focused on a particular service or area of expertise. Municipalities are not expected to participate in every technical discussion.

### What Municipalities Gain

Participation gives municipalities an opportunity to:

- Compare operational questions with those raised by other cities
- Improve how municipal needs are communicated to technical communities
- Influence how existing standards and data models are assessed
- Identify where important meaning or context may be missing
- Reduce ambiguity when discussing requirements with suppliers and integrators
- Contribute to a reusable approach for analyzing interoperability across municipal services

The initiative does not promise immediate compatibility between every platform or vendor. It provides a structured way to identify what must be understood consistently before practical interoperability can be achieved.

### An Open Invitation to Municipalities

The Smart Cities SIG is an open collaborative initiative launched by Madrid City Council and facilitated by the Open Mobile Alliance. Participation is free of charge.

Municipalities of any size and level of technical maturity are invited to contribute. The most valuable starting point is not knowledge of a particular standard. It is knowledge of how a municipal service operates and which questions must be answered to manage it effectively.

By contributing these questions, municipalities can help ensure that interoperability work begins with the reality of public services—not only with the capabilities of available technologies.

### Get Involved

Municipalities and organizations interested in participating can learn more through the [Smart Cities SIG website](https://smartcities-sig-web.vercel.app/) and request access to the [Smart Cities SIG Groups.io community](https://groups.io/g/smartcities-sig).

Once approved, participants gain access to group discussions, meeting information, and collaboration resources. Contributors who also want to participate through GitHub should include their GitHub username when requesting access so that the appropriate repository permissions can be arranged.
