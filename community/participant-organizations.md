## Madrid Positioning 

[Madrid](https://www.madrid.es/digitalcapital), through the [Digital Office](https://www.madrid.es/madriddigitalcapital) of the Madrid City Council, is actively deploying IoT and 5G technologies across city services to improve interoperability and cybersecurity. Participants in this activity include [Polytechnic University of Madrid](https://www.upm.es/internacional), [OMA](https://www.openmobilealliance.org/), [AIOTI](https://aioti.eu/cities-5-0/), among others. It is aligned with initiatives like [Interoperable-europe](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/rolling-plan-ict-standardisation/rolling-plan-2026) and [EIRA/EGovEra](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/european-interoperability-reference-architecture-eira/solution/egovera-business-agnostic-0).

The [Madrid City Council](https://www.madrid.es/digitalcapital) participates in the [Smart Cities SIG](https://github.com/smartcities-sig/smartcities-sig) as:

- A public administration undergoing a digital transformation process.
- An organization committed to delivering the highest-quality services to its citizens.
- A facilitator of open ecosystem collaboration, working with the [Polytechnic University of Madrid](https://iotmadlab.es/en/).

Madrid's primary interest in the initiative is to bridge real municipal interoperability needs with a coherent [digital twin](https://gemelo.madrid.es/en/) supported by a selected set of standards, using a practical, collaborative approach to analyze needs, identify the best available solutions, and carry out testing and validation activities.

Madrid views the Smart Cities SIG as a practical environment for interoperability coordination and requirements incubation, where ecosystem participants can collaboratively:

- Analyze interoperability challenges, where city experts can contribute their knowledge of how the digital twin should represent reality.
- Refine reusable interoperability requirements, including the data models needed to represent the real world, maintain historical data linked to city spaces rather than devices, compare information collected by different technologies, and preserve the same semantics across all layers in an [EIRA/EgovERA context](https://cartool-ec.github.io/SmartMad-Mercamadrid/index.html).
- Validate reusable approaches by deploying them in real [Smart Urban Spaces](https://iotmadlab.es/el-laboratorio/#espacios-urbanos-inteligentes), and support a [catalog of devices](https://iotmadlab.es/dispositivos/) for city-service providers, as well as a [catalog of data models](https://github.com/smart-data-models/incubated/tree/master/SMARTSENSORING/OMA-UCIFI) to help manufacturers understand the needs defined in the cities' mid- and long-term roadmap.

Madrid believes that direct collaboration with other municipalities and the wider ecosystem of incumbents, grounded in a shared and well-defined interpretation, will help everyone create long-term value.

The collaboration methodology proposed for the Smart Cities SIG may contribute to:

- Specifying real needs and translating them into reusable interoperability profiles.
- Defining semantic mappings from real-world operations into digital twins.
- Ensuring semantic continuity by incorporating context through [Smart Data Models](https://smartdatamodels.org/) and [NGSI-LD](https://ngsi-ld.org/).
- Providing interoperability guidance based on collaboration at every level.
- Developing reusable validation methodologies through a dual-step process: first in the laboratory, such as [IoTMADLab](https://iotmadlab.es/en/), and then in real city settings, such as [Smart Urban Spaces](https://iotmadlab.es/el-laboratorio/#espacios-urbanos-inteligentes).

Madrid supports the initiative by providing coordination based on these principles:

- Transparency.
- Citizen-centricity.
- Openness.
- Implementation neutrality.
- Practical ecosystem collaboration.

Madrid views the **Smart Cities SIG** as a collaborative ecosystem initiative intended to complement, not compete with, existing standards organizations, ecosystem alliances, interoperability programs, and industry initiatives.


## AIOTI Positioning

The [Alliance for AI, IoT and Edge Continuum Innovation (AIOTI)](https://aioti.eu/) participates in the Smart Cities SIG through its [Cities 5.0 Working Group](https://aioti.eu/cities-5-0/), whose scope covers the selection of standards, interoperability, scalability, energy efficiency and green enablement, smart transportation, e-services, disaster preparedness and resilience, and convergence to Digital Twins.

AIOTI participates in the Smart Cities SIG as:

- a European multi-stakeholder community bringing together cities, industry, SMEs, startups, research and academia,
- a bridge between municipal needs, standardisation activities, and EU research and innovation initiatives,
- and a channel for replicating validated interoperability approaches across European cities.

The Cities 5.0 WG shares the SIG's core principle: **data is the critical asset; devices are merely tools**. Since *"a digital twin is what you feed it"*, AIOTI prioritises data quality, a shared semantic context for every data item in use (with traceback), and a *"testing before investing"* approach, which is significantly more cost-effective than correcting deployments afterwards.

AIOTI proposes to contribute to the Smart Cities SIG through:

- **Harmonisation across architectural layers:** Supporting the alignment of low-level object standardisation (e.g., [OMA SpecWorks](https://www.openmobilealliance.org/) LwM2M objects) with high-level frameworks (e.g., FIWARE [Smart Data Models](https://smartdatamodels.org/)) and [AIOTI's High-Level Architecture (HLA)](https://aioti.eu/aioti-high-level-architecture-hla-report-r7/), advancing a Solution Architecture Template based on the [eGovERA](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/european-interoperability-reference-architecture-eira/solution/egovera-business-agnostic-0) reference architecture, and addressing the coexistence of legacy and new device generations.
- **Cross-group expertise:** Connecting the SIG with relevant AIOTI horizontal groups, notably Standardisation and [Testing and Experimentation Environments (TEE)](https://aioti.eu/tee/), in line with the SIG's laboratory-to-field validation approach.
- **Replicability and critical mass:** Helping turn SIG outcomes into a replicable model for multiple cities rather than a single-city solution. Only a critical mass of cities expressing shared requirements will achieve a market response to their needs; otherwise, cities will deploy what others decide to manufacture.
- **From use cases to challenges and EU initiatives:** Transforming municipal use cases into challenges for companies and startups — ensuring there is at least one interested client — and, when mature, into EU initiatives fostering interoperability, including consortia for EU-funded projects and common activities with ongoing Horizon Europe projects.
- **Real-world convergence (TRL 7–9):** Promoting the confluence of approaches into effective and efficient IoT deployments in cities: not just another standard, but a selection of solutions demonstrated in real use cases.
- **Dissemination and knowledge transfer:** Sharing SIG results across the AIOTI community, feeding identified gaps into European standardisation mapping efforts (such as the joint AIOTI / StandICT.eu [IoT Standardisation Gaps Report](https://aioti.eu/wp-content/uploads/StandICT.eu-IoT-Standardisation-Gaps-Release-2.pdf)), and supporting organised knowledge transfer to traditional city service providers, so that municipal organisations can absorb high-volume, high-quality data and manage by results rather than by efforts.

AIOTI supports the initiative on the basis of interoperability, scalability, cybersecurity, openness, implementation neutrality, and public-private collaboration.

AIOTI views the **Smart Cities SIG** as a collaborative ecosystem initiative that complements, rather than competes with, existing standards organizations, ecosystem alliances, interoperability programs, and industry initiatives — helping European cities protect long-term investments through human-centric, sustainable, open, and interoperable city services.

OMA views the Smart Cities SIG as a collaborative ecosystem initiative intended to complement existing standards organizations, ecosystem alliances, interoperability programs, and industry initiatives rather than compete with them.

## FIWARE TSC and FIWARE Foundation positioning

FIWARE ([website](https://fiware.org/), [github](https://github.com/FIWARE)) brings a suite of open source components, implementing open standards, which can be picked to be integrated together and/or with other components from third parties to build platforms that ease the creation of smart applications and the digital transformation of organizations into smart organizations playing an active role in the Data Economy (smart cities, smart factories, smart farms, smart logistics operators, etc.). Development of FIWARE started in 2011 with the first companies delivering commercial products based on FIWARE already in 2015\. Since then, [**400+ cities in 35+ countries worldwide**](https://fiware.org/about-us/smart-cities/fiware4cities-book/) **have deployed smart city solutions or built their entire smart city architecture based on FIWARE**.

Any software architecture “powered by FIWARE” is built around management of digital twin data. FIWARE provides open source implementations of the ETSI NGSI-LD API ([website](https://ngsi-ld.org/), [specs](https://www.etsi.org/standards/#search=Core%20API&startDate=2026-01-15&&keyword=NGSI-LD)) enabling management of and access to digital twin data. The combination of the NGSI-LD API together with data models published under the Smart Data Models initiative ([website](https://smartdatamodels.org/), [github](https://github.com/smart-data-models)) provides the basis for interoperability of smart solutions within a city (Air Quality Management, Traffic Control, Waste Management, Public Lighting, etc.) and their replication/portability across multiple cities.

The FIWARE Technical Steering Committee (TSC) is the body within the FIWARE Community that governs the technical direction of FIWARE. FIWARE Foundation, on the other hand, is a non-profit association which brings together members of the FIWARE Community and gives them support in their activities. For example, it supports the coordination of the Smart Data Models initiative.

Members of the FIWARE Community, the FIWARE TSC and the FIWARE Foundation participate actively in the Smart Cities SIG and adhere to its methodology with the aim of:

- driving identification and further curation of smart data models driven by city needs  
- supporting identification of OMA objects that may feed smart digital twin-based systems using smart data models

This will support practical materialization of standards-based software architectures for smart cities based on FIWARE, implementing an effective end-to-end bidirectional pipeline between OMA-compliant devices and context broker implementations managing the digital twin data representation of the city.

## Paradox Engineering SA Positioning

**Headquartered in Switzerland, [Paradox Engineering](https://www.pdxeng.ch/)** designs and develops open-standard IoT network technologies, with smart lighting as main focus. Since the beginning, the company portfolio has been having interoperability at its core, under the clear vision that truly future-proof urban digital infrastructures can only be enabled by open, interoperable, standards-based solutions.

Paradox Engineering will contribute to the Smart Cities SIG through:

- **Data Model Validation:** Applying Paradox Engineering's extensive R&D expertise to refine and validate the initiative's data model.
- **Lighting & IoT Domain Expertise:** Leveraging Paradox Engineering's long-standing, global experience in designing, developing and deploying standards-based smart lighting and IoT solutions.
- **Device Interoperability Testing:** Utilizing Paradox Engineering's lighting controllers to rigorously test system performance and data model adherence.

The primary objective in this initiative is to demonstrate the tangible, real-world value that interoperability delivers to municipalities and utilities — enabling them to build resilient, future-proof, and highly adaptive smart city ecosystems, built to last throughout innovations in technology and evolutions of needs. Paradox Engineering is fully committed to serving as a trusted, experienced voice, helping cities build simple, reliable and secure IoT infrastructures.

## SEAMWARE Positioning

SEAMWARE (Seamless Middleware Technologies SL) is a non-profit company that was established to contribute to the sustainability of open source software projects in general, with an initial focus on key projects related to FIWARE ([website](https://fiware.org/), [github](https://github.com/FIWARE)).

Currently, the company focuses on:

- research and development projects that accelerate the FIWARE roadmap (the evolution of existing components and the development of new ones) and/or the adoption of FIWARE across different application domains,  
- the provision of training, technical support and ongoing enhancement services for FIWARE components (see the section later in this document).

SEAMWARE brings together the lead developers of key FIWARE software components (Orion-LD and Careine, implementing the ETSI NGSI-LD, advanced IAM based on Verifiable Credentials, Data Space Components, etc) as well as the coordination of the community-driven Smart Data Model initiative  ([website](https://smartdatamodels.org/), [github](https://github.com/smart-data-models)).

SEAMWARE welcomes creation of the Smart Cities SIG as it will help to define a complete Interoperability Profile that cities can take as reference for materializing their Smart City architecture integrating smart solutions from different vendors using a Digital Twin-based System-of-Systems approach.

SEAMWARE will contribute their expertise on Smart Data Models to the Smart Cities SIG and wide experience materializing digital twin-based smart cities powered by FIWARE.

## Libelium Positioning

**Libelium** develops IoT sensing and software solutions for smart cities and infrastructure, connecting physical-world observations with urban data platforms, data spaces, and digital twins. Through its sensing technologies and platform, Libelium brings an end-to-end perspective on how data is generated, managed, shared, and transformed into actionable information for municipalities.

Libelium participates in the **Smart Cities SIG** as an IoT device manufacturer and platform provider, a contributor of practical implementation experience, and an advocate for open standards, trustworthy data, and implementation-neutral ecosystem collaboration. Libelium has also been a key promoter for a long time of OMA LwM2M with reserved objects group and a key promoter and contributor of the Smart Data Models. At the same time, the company is member of the FIWARE Foundation, with a key role in the Board of Directors.

Libelium’s primary interest in the initiative is to help municipalities achieve **interoperability from physical measurement to digital representation**. Its position is that successful data exchange should preserve not only a value and its format, but also what was observed, where and when the observation occurred, how it was obtained, and whether it is suitable for the intended municipal service.

Libelium proposes to contribute to the Smart Cities SIG through:

- **Device-to-platform semantic interoperability:** Support the identification and validation of mappings between **OMA LwM2M objects**, relevant **IPSO/uCIFI models**, **Smart Data Models**, and **NGSI-LD** representations. The objective is to preserve operational meaning across device, platform, and application boundaries, while documenting mapping assumptions and gaps rather than assuming that different models are automatically equivalent. This aligns with the SIG’s approach of mapping municipal requirements to existing standards and ecosystem initiatives.
- **Data quality and measurement comparability:** Help define reusable requirements for measurement units, calibration metadata, uncertainty, provenance, sampling and aggregation periods, completeness, freshness, and quality flags. Libelium advocates treating data quality as part of interoperability: municipalities should be able to assess whether observations from different devices and technologies are comparable and fit for a particular purpose, not merely whether their messages can be exchanged. Libelium leads a ISO 17025 compliance laboratory with the capacity to certify data quality for IoT devices, calibration and validation. Including key works about data quality representation over Smart Data Models [https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-29430-2\_25](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-29430-2_25 "https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-29430-2_25")
- **Spatial and historical continuity:** Contribute approaches that distinguish the **sensor**, the **observation**, and the **real-world entity or space being observed**. The aim is to preserve the history of a street, building, water asset, or urban space when devices are replaced, relocated, or supplied by different manufacturers, while retaining traceability to the original observations. This directly supports the spatially anchored historical-data requirements identified in Madrid’s positioning.
- **Practical, multi-vendor validation:** Contribute representative sensing scenarios, sample datasets, mapping examples, and reproducible test cases. Libelium supports a laboratory-to-field validation approach, assessing semantic consistency and data quality alongside technical connectivity, and documenting findings that other municipalities and suppliers can reuse. This is consistent with the validation approach described by Madrid and IoTMADLab.
- **Reusable data for digital twins and data spaces:** Draw on the integration of IoT, data spaces, and digital twins to explore how validated observations can support multiple municipal services without losing their meaning, provenance, or usage conditions.

Libelium supports applying these contributions to the SIG’s initial **public street lighting, smart water metering** activities, with additional sensing domains considered according to municipality-defined priorities and the group’s agreed scope such as **irrigation management, noise monitoring, environmental monitoring, air quality and parking.**

Libelium believes that cities should be able to **replace devices, combine suppliers, evolve platforms, and reuse data without losing operational meaning or historical continuity**. It therefore supports transparency, openness, cybersecurity, data sovereignty, implementation neutrality, and validation grounded in real municipal needs.

Libelium views the **Smart Cities SIG** as a collaborative environment for translating operational experience into shared interoperability requirements, semantic mappings, and validation guidance. Its purpose should be to complement existing standards organizations and ecosystem initiatives, helping municipalities build trustworthy, adaptable digital infrastructures that deliver lasting value to citizens.
